# ISEE Arcade Curriculum Blueprint

This document governs the generic Kindergarten–Grade 8 learning routes and the
three ISEE test-prep routes. It exists to prevent accidental grade bleed,
standards drift, thin banks hidden behind randomized numbers, and generic
machine-generated vocabulary.

## Non-negotiable separation

- The existing ISEE Lower Level source remains protected.
- ISEE Lower, Middle, and Upper follow ERB formats and section blueprints.
- Generic grade routes are a broader curriculum. Science, history, civics,
  engineering, arts/design, idioms, puns, and figurative language belong here.
- Grade-only enrichment must never enter an ISEE timed section merely because
  both happen to use a `verbal` or `reading` question type.
- Appropriate vocabulary overlap is allowed. Forced disjointness is not a
  learning goal; correct level, sense, context, and review spacing are.

## Source framework

| Area | Primary guidance | How it is used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ISEE | ERB current preparation pages and What to Expect guides | Section format, counts, timing, verbal item types, reading skills, and level boundaries |
| ELA | Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts | Grade progression for vocabulary, roots and affixes, context, figurative language, reading evidence, writing, and grammar |
| General vocabulary | `wordfreq` corpus rankings and Princeton WordNet | Frequency calibration, base forms, senses, and original answer construction |
| Middle-school academic vocabulary | Greene and Coxhead Middle School Vocabulary Lists | Cross-curricular terms from English, health, mathematics, science, and social studies |
| Higher academic vocabulary | New Academic Word List | Upper middle-school and advanced academic headword candidates |
| Science and engineering | Next Generation Science Standards | Grade topics, science practices, engineering design, models, evidence, systems, and crosscutting concepts |
| Space science | NASA Learning Resources and NASA/JPL Education | Current, grade-banded astronomy, solar-system, Earth/space, engineering, and mission contexts |
| Social studies | NCSS C3 Framework | Civics, economics, geography, history, inquiry, source evaluation, evidence, and informed action |

The word-list sources guide headword selection. The app does not copy commercial
test-prep questions, proprietary explanations, or source examples.

### Idiom item contract

The idiom bank follows the [Common Core Grade 3 Language standards][ccss-l3],
which require sentence-level context and distinguishing literal from nonliteral
meaning in context. Its teaching shape also follows the
[ReadWriteThink grades 3–5 idiom lesson][rwt-idioms]: children encounter an idiom
in a natural sentence, interpret its figurative meaning, and receive an
explanation that connects the context to that meaning.

Every idiom item must:

- show a complete context sentence before asking for the meaning;
- ask what the idiom means **in that sentence**, not as isolated trivia;
- have one defensible best answer and three plausible, parallel distractors;
- use distractors based on a likely nearby misunderstanding, not random or silly
  definitions from unrelated phrases;
- explain both the context clue and the difference between figurative and literal
  meaning;
- progress from familiar/context-rich items to meanings that require inference.

The option-writing rules follow published assessment guidance: distractors must
be plausible, similar in form, mutually exclusive, and free of clues. See the
[Australian Curriculum formative-assessment guidance][acara-mcq] and
[TIMSS item-writing guidelines][timss-items].

[ccss-l3]: https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/L/3/
[rwt-idioms]: https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/figurative-language-teaching-idioms
[acara-mcq]: https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/resources/formative-assessment/designing-formative-assessment/multiple-choice-questions
[timss-items]: https://timss2019.org/timssandpirls.bc.edu/timss2019/methods/pdf/T19-item-writing-guidelines.pdf

## Breadth targets

- Every generic grade route: at least 500 vocabulary/word-meaning exposures.
- Grades 3–8: at least 500 fully usable vocabulary questions after quality
  review, not merely 500 scraped strings.
- Every grade and ISEE route: at least 200 non-vocabulary question families.
- A family is a distinct skill shape or fixed content item. Changing only the
  numbers does not turn one family into twenty.
- Question selection cycles through the whole eligible pool before repetition
  whenever practical.

## Grade progression

### Kindergarten

- ELA: spoken vocabulary, rhyme, beginning sounds, print awareness, story order.
- Math: counting, comparing groups, shapes, position, simple patterns.
- Science/STEAM: pushes and pulls, weather, living things' needs, observing and
  describing, building with simple materials.
- Social studies: self, family, classroom rules, helpers, basic map language.
- Space: noticing the Sun, Moon, stars, day/night, and observable sky patterns.
- Figurative language: literal versus silly/pretend meaning through narration
  and pictures; no abstract terminology required.

### First Grade

- ELA: decoding, common word meanings, sentence meaning, story details.
- Math: addition/subtraction within 20, place value, time, measurement.
- Science/STEAM: light, sound, plant/animal structures, seasonal sky patterns,
  asking questions and comparing designs.
- Social studies: past/present, community roles, needs/wants, simple maps.
- Space: day/night patterns, Moon observations, the Sun as a star.
- Figurative language: playful double meanings, sound words, very familiar
  nonliteral phrases.

### Second Grade

- ELA: fluency, context clues, prefixes/suffixes, central message.
- Math: place value, two-digit operations, equal groups, measurement and data.
- Science/STEAM: properties of matter, habitats, pollination/seed movement,
  land and water change, testing simple solutions.
- Social studies: communities, geography, goods/services, basic government.
- Space: planets as worlds, the solar system as a family of objects, scale as
  "nearer/farther" rather than numerical astrophysics.
- Figurative language: familiar idioms, literal/nonliteral meaning, simple puns.

### Third Grade

- ELA: multisyllabic words, roots/affixes, main idea, evidence, point of view.
- Math: multiplication/division, fractions, area/perimeter, scaled graphs.
- Science/STEAM: forces, ecosystems, life cycles/traits, weather and climate,
  fair tests and design constraints.
- Social studies: local/state/community history, maps, resources, trade,
  civic participation and multiple perspectives.
- Space: solar-system members, relative size/distance models, gravity as a pull.
- Figurative language: idioms in context, similes, metaphors, personification,
  hyperbole, proverbs, and puns based on homophones or multiple meanings.

### Fourth Grade

- ELA: theme, inference, text structure, precise words, morphology.
- Math: multi-digit operations, fraction equivalence, angles, measurement/data.
- Science/STEAM: energy transfer, waves, information processing, weathering and
  erosion, prototype testing, art/design communication.
- Social studies: U.S. regions and Indigenous histories, colonization in
  context, geography, state/federal basics, evidence from primary sources.
- Space: solar-system scale models, Saturn and its moons/rings, light and
  telescopes at a conceptual level.
- Figurative language: comparison of simile/metaphor, idiom meaning, imagery,
  personification, alliteration, onomatopoeia, puns, and proverbs.

### Fifth Grade

- ELA: evidence across texts, figurative meaning, academic vocabulary, writing.
- Math: fraction operations, decimals, volume, coordinate plane, data.
- Science/STEAM: matter, ecosystems and energy, Earth systems, stars and the
  solar system, criteria/constraints and controlled tests.
- Social studies: broad U.S. history foundations, constitutional principles,
  branches of government, rights/responsibilities, geography and economics.
- Space: the Sun as a star, Earth/Moon/Sun patterns, brightness and distance,
  planetary properties, models and the limits of models.
- Figurative language: idioms, metaphor, simile, symbolism, imagery, hyperbole,
  proverbs, wordplay, connotation, and explaining effect on a reader.

### Sixth Grade

- ELA: argument/evidence, text structure, roots, connotation, source comparison.
- Math: ratios, rational numbers, expressions/equations, statistics, geometry.
- Science/STEAM: matter, energy, forces, waves, cells/ecosystems, Earth systems,
  engineering tradeoffs and data analysis.
- Social studies: ancient/world civilizations, physical/human geography,
  markets and scarcity, civic institutions, source reliability.
- Space: Earth-Sun-Moon models, seasons/eclipses, gravity and orbits, scale.
- Figurative language: nuanced idioms, analogy, symbolism, allusion, irony,
  puns, connotation/denotation, tone, and figurative-language analysis.

### Seventh Grade

- ELA: close reading, argument quality, language choices, multiple sources.
- Math: proportional relationships, equations, geometry, probability.
- Science/STEAM: chemical reactions, forces/energy, ecosystems, heredity and
  natural selection, iterative engineering and computational thinking.
- Social studies: world history and cultural exchange, governance systems,
  trade, migration, rights, civic discourse, corroborating sources.
- Space: orbital motion, gravity and mass, electromagnetic spectrum, solar
  activity, planetary geology and habitable conditions.
- Figurative language: idiom/register, extended metaphor, irony types,
  euphemism, understatement, oxymoron, satire basics, sophisticated puns.

### Eighth Grade

- ELA: rhetoric, claims/evidence/reasoning, style, ambiguity, advanced roots.
- Math: linear relationships, functions, transformations, exponents, data.
- Science/STEAM: integrated middle-school physics, chemistry, biology, Earth
  science, engineering optimization, uncertainty and model evaluation.
- Social studies: U.S. history and constitutional government, elections and
  institutions, federalism, civil liberties, economics, media/source literacy.
- Space/astrophysics: spectra and light, stellar formation/evolution, galaxies,
  cosmic scale, gravity, evidence for universe models, black holes conceptually.
- Figurative language: symbolism, motif, allusion, paradox, irony, satire,
  euphemism, ambiguity, rhetorical analogy, puns, tone and authorial effect.

## Civics and politics guardrails

- Content is nonpartisan and fact-centered.
- Questions teach institutions, processes, constitutional principles, evidence,
  historical context, and how to compare claims.
- Current officeholders, live election results, and changing laws require
  current authoritative sourcing before publication.
- A defensible answer must not depend on agreement with a political viewpoint.

## Content quality gates

- No profanity, slurs, sexual definitions, adult substance references, or
  demeaning dictionary senses in child banks.
- No proper names accidentally treated as general vocabulary.
- No alternate spelling presented as a synonym.
- No answer whose explanation uses a different sense of the word.
- Definitions must be understandable at the selected grade, not merely correct.
- Science/history answers require a source record and a last-reviewed date.
- Generated questions are sampled across the full bank, not just the first page.
- ISEE audits explicitly test for generic-grade and enrichment leakage.
